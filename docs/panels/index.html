Panels

OpenCue is built from 22 panels that you place freely. Here is every panel type with a picture and what it does – add them through Edit → Add Panel and combine them into your own layouts.

Programming

DMX Monitor

Shows the DMX values of the universes live.

Exactly what is being sent, channel by channel and universe by universe. The patched fixtures are outlined, so you can find a channel at a glance – the quickest way to check a patch or to see why a light does not react.

Fixture List

List of patched fixtures: select, patch, edit.

The patch table with one row per fixture: number, name, type, universe, address and channel count. Add fixtures from the library (GDTF files included), re-patch or rename them, and select them for the other panels.

Fixture Control

Set values of the selected fixtures (dimmer, color, position ...).

The programmer. Every channel of a fixture gets its own fader, sorted into dimmer, position, colour, beam and focus, plus colour pickers (RGB, HSV, CMY) and direct DMX access. The tile grid selects the fixtures you work on.

Variables / Effects

Recall stored variables and effects quickly.

Store colours, positions and beams as named variables and sort them into groups. A variable is a live reference: change it once and every cue and fader that uses it follows. Effects are recalled from the same panel.

Effect Studio

Design effects: curves, speed, offset.

Write your own effects in a small C++/Arduino-like script language, with a live preview of the curve and built-in help. Effects can run at their own rate and can react to the audio analysis.

Cues and playback

Cue List

Create, edit and play cue lists.

Store, update, edit and play the cues of a list. Each cue has fade times, a trigger (manual, timecode, clock, audio and more) and MIB. Select several cells to edit or copy them at once; a global undo takes mistakes back.

Executor (cue playback)

Run one cue list with fader, Go and pause.

Plays a cue list with Go, Pause, Back and Stop. The mix fader blends between your manual values and the cue, so you can bring a look in under your own hand.

Input and control

Command Line

Type and run commands.

Type commands like Fixture 1 Thru 4 At 80. Tab completes names, the input is checked while you type, and the history keeps what you ran. Cue lists can be addressed by name.

Command Numpad

Number pad for entering commands by touch.

Enter commands without a keyboard: large keys for numbers, Thru, At and the confirm key (Please) – made for tablets and touch screens.

Command Buttons

Buttons for frequently used commands.

One tap for Fixture, Group, Store, Go, Blackout and the other everyday commands – a good partner for the keypad on a tablet.

Button

Your own button that runs a command.

Give it a name and a command such as Go Cue 1 or Blackout and build your own surface. Label, size and colour are yours to set, and you can place as many buttons as you need.

Fader

Your own fader, for example for a dimmer or master.

Use {value} in the command, for example Fixture 1 Thru 4 At {value}. Set the range, label and starting value, and place faders for groups, masters or anything else a command can set.

Toggle New

On/off switch with its own command for each state.

A switch with two commands: one runs when you switch it on, the other when you switch it off. Name it, colour it and choose its starting state – handy for haze, strobe or house lights.

Simple Control New

A simple control surface for lights – made for handing over to someone else.

Fixture tiles sorted by group, a brightness fader, a colour field with colour buttons, a spring-loaded joystick for pan and tilt, zoom, gobos, blackout and ready-made looks (rainbow, circle, pulse, strobe). Made for operator mode – everything else stays out of reach.

Media and time

Timecode

Global timeline with start, stop, speed and cue triggering.

Run, pause and jump, and change the speed from 0.1× to 8×. Cues with the trigger Timecode start by themselves at their time; LTC and MIDI timecode can serve as sources.

Timeline Editor New

Cues and commands as markers on a graphical time axis, with the waveforms of your songs.

A graphical timeline: one track per cue list with markers for every cue that triggers on timecode, command tracks, and audio tracks with the waveforms of your songs. Drag markers to retime them, use “Set cue here” to place the current cue, and open the panel as its own window.

Audio New

Live audio analysis: spectrum, beat and tempo for effects that react to music.

Listens to the system sound, an input or the built-in demo beat and shows a 32-band spectrum, kick, snare and hi-hat lamps, the level and the tempo (automatic, tap, half/double or manual). The results are variables such as a_level, a_bass and a_kick that effects and cues can use – 33 ready-made audio effects are included.

Video/Audio Player

Plays video or audio, optionally in sync with the timecode.

Add video, audio or image files to your show and play them in sync with the timecode or freely. Loop, mute, set the volume and fit the picture – the same songs appear as waveforms in the timeline editor.

Livestream / Camera

Shows a livestream (camera address) or a camera attached to the main device.

Show IP cameras by MJPEG, HLS or video URL, or a camera on the main computer, with a full-screen view. Stream passwords are stored encrypted on your computer.

Stage and assistant

3D Simulation

Shows the stage in 3D, with the light of your fixtures.

Light cones, light pools, gobos and strobe follow the DMX signal live. Build a stage from simple shapes or your own STL/OBJ models, hang the fixtures with their GDTF models, and save stages with undo and redo.

AI Assistant New

Chat with a language model that runs on your own computer – nothing leaves it.

Talks to a local Ollama model – nothing is sent to the internet. The modes Chat, Guide and Work answer questions, explain the program or do the work for you, and you decide per kind of action whether it may read, write or has to ask first. Chats are saved as files.

Chat & Notes New

Chat with the other devices and keep notes and checklists – saved on the server.

A messenger and a notebook in one. Chat with the iPads, phones and the console that are connected to OpenCue – every message shows who wrote it, appears at once on every device and stays saved on the server. Next to the chats you can write notes and checklists in Markdown, with a toolbar for bold, underline, lists and checkboxes; in the finished view you tick the checkboxes directly. Nothing leaves your network.
